# Latest Prior-Art Watch — 2026-08-29

## Newly hardened boundaries

1. **Context-dependent threshold decryption (ASIACRYPT 2025 / ePrint 2025/279):** decryption shares can be isolated by a cryptographic context. KEYSTONE therefore does not claim context binding or share-isolation as new. The current MPP uses context-bound proof transcripts; a production paper must distinguish that from full context-dependent threshold decryption.
2. **UQ-Vote (IEEE Access 14, 128905--128916, DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3725080):** consensus-finalized authorization, public bulletin-board logging, threshold-share release, and liveness/fault evaluation already appear in an adjacent voting setting. Crossref and OpenAlex metadata converge; refresh the publisher record before submission.

## Narrow working KEYSTONE differentiator

The broad novelty claim is not defensible. Pending patent, standards-adjacent,
and citation-chain closure, the only claim-safe candidate is:

> KEYSTONE separates ciphertext data availability from present dispute-key
> serviceability in an encrypted-rollup dispute workflow, then studies a
> pre-authorization, non-production canary partial-decryption audit with
> explicit catastrophic false-accept analysis, temporal and correlated-failure
> qualifications, and a deadline-evidence interface under declared timing
> assumptions.

This is a property, composition, and analysis hypothesis. It is not a claim of
a new threshold-encryption, DLEQ, context-dependent decryption, proactive
sharing, bulletin-board, slashing, data-availability, or key-release primitive.

Selective-withholding experiments and the reproducible prototype are supporting
evaluation artifacts. They are not separate novelty claims, and successful
canary audits must not be presented as proof of future dispute cooperation.
