Abstract
Encrypted optimistic rollups may preserve the availability of ciphertext and execution receipts while failing
when a dispute requires timely authorized decryption. This paper studies that gap as dispute-key availability
(DKA): the conditional ability of an authorized verifier to obtain at least a threshold number of valid decryption
contributions before a dispute deadline, while preserving confidentiality before authorization. KEYSTONE
is a non-production cryptographic-systems design that combines established threshold-KEM and verifiable
partial-decryption components with an unpredictable canary audit, a public request/deadline transcript, and
failure-domain-aware sampling. For a committee of n custodians, threshold t, audit sample size s, and
acceptance threshold q, we derive a hypergeometric false-accept expression for static catastrophic states. At

Introduction

Private or encrypted optimistic execution introduces an operational dependency that public data-availability
mechanisms do not remove. A ciphertext can remain retrievable while the threshold service needed to open its
record key is unavailable, late, invalid, or selectively uncooperative. In an optimistic dispute, that difference is
decisive: the system must recover enough valid contributions after authorization and before a deadline, not merely
prove that encrypted bytes exist.
KEYSTONE studies this distinction in the setting of encrypted AI-rollup disputes. The system keeps each
inference record encrypted under a fresh symmetric key and wraps that key under an epoch threshold public key.
A routine audit samples custodians using post-commit randomness and asks each sampled custodian to operate
on a canonical, non-production group element. A valid response demonstrates current access to the custodian’s
epoch share and the ability to perform the required partial-decryption operation. An authorized dispute instead
requests partial decryptions of the target record’s threshold-KEM component. These two paths share cryptographic
machinery but measure different behavior.
The central research question is:
1

How can an encrypted AI rollup obtain publicly auditable, non-revealing evidence that at least t valid
threshold-decryption contributions are currently serviceable for an authorized dispute before deadline ∆,
under explicit churn and correlated-failure assumptions, without releasing a production key or plaintext
before authorization?
The proposed contribution is deliberately narrow:
1. a decomposition that separates ciphertext availability, valid-share registration, audit-time readiness, authorized
decryptability, and dispute deadline liveness;
2. a non-production canary audit constructed from established threshold-decryption and equality-of-discrete-log
proof techniques;
3. a closed-form false-accept bound for a static catastrophic readiness state, with explicit limits on repeated-audit
inference; and
4. an executable internal prototype and adversarial simulator that expose both useful evidence and a selectivewithholding failure boundary.
The paper does not claim a new threshold-encryption, VSS/PVSS, DLEQ, proactive-sharing, context-dependentdecryption, or bulletin-board primitive. It also does not claim that a successful audit proves unconditional future
availability, adaptive selective-withholding resistance, or production security.

Background and Related Work

Data-availability sampling, including PeerDAS, concerns the retrievability of encoded public data and is therefore
a contrast class rather than a solution to threshold-key serviceability [Ryan et al., 2024]. Threshold encryption
establishes that a ciphertext can be opened only with sufficiently many decryption contributions; classical and
modern constructions supply the confidentiality and reconstruction substrate adopted here [Boneh et al., 2006].
Verifiable secret sharing, proactive sharing, and dynamic proactive secret sharing address correct distribution,
refresh, and changing committees, but do not by themselves measure whether a dispute service will meet a future
deadline [Schoenmakers, 1999, Herzberg et al., 1995, Yurek et al., 2023].
Threshold-release systems already cover important parts of the design space. ETHTID studies deployable
threshold information disclosure on Ethereum; Ferveo and BEAT-MEV apply threshold decryption to encrypted
transaction ordering; vetKeys and time-lock encrypted storage address controlled blockchain disclosure; and
context-dependent threshold decryption provides established context-isolation machinery [Stengele et al., 2021,
Bebel and Ojha, 2022, Bormet et al., 2025, Cerulli et al., 2023, Agarwal et al., 2025, Boneh et al., 2025]. EigenAI
provides adjacent encrypted/verifiable AI execution context rather than evidence for the present availability claim
[Alves et al., 2026]. Accountability for threshold-decryption misbehavior and accountable liveness also constrain
what public evidence can establish [Boneh et al., 2023, Lewis-Pye et al., 2025].
The bounded prior-art review therefore rejects broad primitive novelty. The surviving research hypothesis is
whether the exact rollup-dispute framing—ciphertext/serviceability separation, pre-authorization canary exercise,
explicit catastrophic false-accept analysis, correlated-failure qualification, and deadline evidence—constitutes a
materially useful systems contribution. Absence of a single combined predecessor in the bounded search is not
proof of novelty.

System and Threat Model

3.1

Entities and records

An epoch E contains n custodians and a threshold t. Custodian i holds share xi and registers public share Yi = g xi .
A rollup worker encrypts record r using a fresh symmetric key Kr and an authenticated-encryption scheme. The
worker wraps Kr through a threshold Diffie–Hellman KEM under the epoch public key P KE and publishes the
encrypted record and receipt to a data-availability layer. The settlement layer records the relevant commitment,
policy, request, and deadline events.
The model includes the rollup worker, data-availability layer, custodian committee, randomness beacon, audit
coordinator, policy/dispute contract, authorized verifier/combiner, and adjudication logic. The ledger supplies an
ordered public clock. Cryptographic evidence for an invalid proof or equivocation is positive evidence; attributing a
missed deadline to malicious behavior additionally requires a declared synchrony and delivery assumption.

Readiness and security properties

For request type u ∈ {audit, dispute}, define

iff custodian i can access its valid current share, execute the required operation, generate a valid proof, and deliver
its response by τ + ∆. Audit and dispute readiness are intentionally different: a target-aware custodian may answer
a canary but refuse a particular dispute.
The relevant properties are:
Ciphertext availability (CA).
The encrypted record and receipt bytes are retrievable from the data-availability layer.
Valid-share registration.
The registered shares are consistent with one epoch threshold key according to the adopted DKG/VSS/PVSS layer.
Audit-time key-service readiness (AKR).
At least q members of a sampled set return valid, context-bound canary partial decryptions by the audit deadline.
Authorized decryptability.
Following valid authorization, an approved combiner obtains at least t distinct valid partial decryptions of the
target record component.
Dispute deadline liveness (DDL).
Authorized decryptability completes by the dispute deadline.
Pre-authorization confidentiality (PAC).
Before authorization, an adversary controlling fewer than t current shares learns no information about the record
key beyond leakage from the base encryption and metadata scheme.
KEYSTONE defines (ε, ∆d )-DKA as the conditional property that, following valid authorization and under the
stated readiness and network assumptions, the combiner obtains t valid contributions by τ + ∆d with probability at
least 1 − ε, while PAC holds before authorization. DKA is thus a deadline-bounded service property rather than an
unconditional promise of future key availability.

3.3

Adversaries and exclusions

The model considers independent crash faults, provider/region/software-domain correlated outages, invalid or
equivocal responses, changing compromise across epochs, selective withholding, beacon prediction or substitution,
and unauthorized requests. Confidentiality assumes fewer than t current shares are compromised. Production
DKG/PVSS security, malicious authorized verifiers, arbitrary asynchronous partitions, and fully adaptive selectivewithholding resistance remain out of scope.

5.2

Three non-implication witnesses

The scientific core is not only the favorable bound. It also contains three constructive limitations:
1. CA does not imply DKA: ciphertext remains available while only t − 1 custodians are dispute-ready.
2. AKR does not imply target-dispute success: selective custodians answer the canary but withhold the target
response.
3. A finite sequence of accepted audits does not imply unconditional future DKA: enough custodians may become
unavailable after the last audit.
These witnesses define what the audit can and cannot support. They prevent an operational health signal from
being presented as a timeless availability proof.

Preliminary findings

The exact baseline calculation gives a 1.9346% false-accept ceiling in the worst static catastrophic state and a
98.0654% detection probability for one audit. Figure 2a shows how detection changes with sample size under the
same static assumptions.
The selective-withholding sweep is a negative result by design. With n = 32 and t = 22, the first deterministic
reconstruction failure occurs at w = n − t + 1 = 11 withholding custodians. At that point only twenty-one target
responses remain. In the internal 4,000-trial cell, audit pass was 1.0 with Wilson interval [0.99904, 1.0], while
reconstruction success was 0.0 with interval [0, 0.000959]. The result is a simulator-bounded witness, not an estimate
of adversarial prevalence.
Local test and benchmark artifacts demonstrate that the proposed interfaces are executable, including threshold
reconstruction, context-bound proofs, canonical transcript serialization, Ed25519 response binding, and bulletinboard state transitions. Those artifacts remain same-workspace engineering evidence. The distributed deadline
family, production DKG/PVSS integration, independently controlled reproduction, and external validation are still
absent.

7

Discussion

The key conceptual result is that availability is layered. Public data availability answers whether encrypted bytes
can be retrieved. Valid-share registration answers whether a threshold key was initially formed consistently. A
readiness audit answers whether sampled custodians can currently perform a non-production share operation. An
authorized dispute asks whether at least t custodians will serve a specific target before a deadline. Treating these
questions as interchangeable hides distinct failure modes.
KEYSTONE’s canary is useful because it exercises the secret-dependent operation without selecting a production
ciphertext. Post-commit beacon sampling reduces advance knowledge of the sampled set; domain-separated context
and proofs bind each response to the registered share and request. However, this evidence is necessarily conditional.
It is strongest against static crash or invalid-response states and weakest against target-aware cooperation changes.
6

Failure-domain metadata creates a second systems question. If every domain contains at most n − t custodians,
loss of one entire domain leaves at least t shares. For n = 32 and t = 22, a sufficient cap is ten shares per domain; the
baseline uses four domains of eight. This rule is only as reliable as the domain labels and independence assumptions.
Domain-stratified sampling can improve visibility into concentrated outages but changes the response-count
distribution and may also increase benign audit failures.
Public deadline records support objective statements about what appeared on the bulletin board and when.
Invalid proofs and equivocation can produce positive cryptographic evidence. Non-response is different: it proves
absence from the canonical transcript, not necessarily malicious intent, unless the network and delivery assumptions
justify attribution. The design should therefore separate warning, temporary inactivity, replacement, and hard
slashing.

8

Limitations and Falsification Conditions

The present work has six material limitations.
1. Novelty remains unresolved. Broad component novelty is defeated by prior work. The narrow composition
must still survive authenticated independent review and a submission-time search refresh.
2. The audit does not prove future cooperation. Selective withholding is an explicit counterexample rather
than a solved problem.
3. Temporal inference is bounded. Repeated static-audit multiplication does not apply to evolving readiness
without a justified temporal model.
4. Deadline performance is unmeasured. No authorized distributed experiment currently establishes end-to-end
success before ∆d .
5. The prototype is non-production. It substitutes a dealer for production DKG/PVSS and has not received
an external cryptographic or smart-contract audit.
6. External validity is absent. Current simulations use synthetic failure models and local execution; they do
not establish provider behavior, deployment reliability, cost, or societal impact.
The narrow research hypothesis should be rejected or reframed if a stronger predecessor already makes the
same dispute-specific property and readiness probe explicit; if the canary measures no operational quantity beyond
generic heartbeat or share registration; if correlated-failure analysis changes no relevant system decision; or if the
authorized confirmatory study fails to reproduce the claimed property separation under its frozen conditions.

9

Conclusion

KEYSTONE isolates a failure mode that ciphertext data availability alone does not address: the threshold
service required for an authorized encrypted-rollup dispute may be unavailable or uncooperative at the deadline.
The proposed framework decomposes the relevant properties, instantiates a non-production canary audit using
established cryptographic components, and derives an explicit static catastrophic false-accept bound. Its strongest
current evidence includes the exact baseline calculation and a limitation-bearing selective-withholding witness. The
defensible claim is therefore conditional and systems-oriented: KEYSTONE measures present sampled serviceability
under declared assumptions; it does not prove future availability or introduce a new threshold-decryption primitive.
Independent novelty review, authorized confirmatory execution, distributed deadline evaluation, and external
reproduction remain necessary before a publication-readiness judgment.
